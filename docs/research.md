<p class="st-kicker">Research</p>
<h1>Timing in neural networks</h1>
<p>I build physics-first models of neural populations and ask how timing shapes what they compute: when they synchronise, how outside rhythms lock them, and how plasticity tunes the delays between them.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p class="st-kicker">Theme 01</p>
<h2>Rhythms and entrainment</h2>
<p>A network that already oscillates does not simply follow an external drive. How easily transcranial alternating current stimulation locks a recurrent E/I spiking network depends on the network's own coherence: weakly synchronised networks entrain over a wider band of frequencies, so their Arnold tongue is wider.</p>
<p>A second line asks what sets the rhythm in the first place. Inhibitory synaptic timescales drive transitions between oscillatory regimes in recurrent E–I networks.</p>
<p class="st-links"><a href="../publications/">Chaos, Solitons &amp; Fractals 2025</a><a href="../publications/">Physical Review Research 2026</a></p>

</section>

<p class="st-kicker">Theme 02</p>
<h2>Delays and plasticity</h2>
<p>Connections between brain areas arrive with a spread of conduction delays. Spike-timing-dependent plasticity acting on those delays prunes the redundant connections and keeps the ones whose timing lets activity propagate from layer to layer, shaping networks that transmit signals efficiently.</p>
<p class="st-links"><a href="../publications/">Journal of Neuroscience 2026</a></p>
</div>
</section>

<p class="st-kicker">Theme 03</p>
<h2>Synchrony on networks</h2>
<p>Kuramoto phase oscillators on a ring, each coupled to its nearest neighbours with delays that grow with distance, split into local synchrony and phase clusters. In two-dimensional spiking networks the same questions become questions about travelling waves.</p>

<h2>Noise as a resource</h2>
<p>Noise can help a neuron detect a weak signal. I study stochastic resonance in Hodgkin-Huxley neurons and networks with delayed feedback, comparing six circuit motifs from a single neuron to a balanced E–I network.</p>
